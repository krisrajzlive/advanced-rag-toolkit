Vector Similarity Metrics for RAG Indexes
===========================================

Cosine similarity measures the cosine of the angle between two vectors,
ignoring their magnitude. It is the most common metric for text embeddings
because sentence embedding models are usually trained with cosine-based
contrastive losses. A cosine similarity of 1.0 means the vectors point in
the same direction; 0.0 means they are orthogonal (unrelated).

Dot product similarity is the raw inner product of two vectors. Unlike
cosine similarity it is sensitive to vector magnitude, so it rewards longer
vectors. It is cheaper to compute than cosine similarity (no normalization
step) and is the natural choice when embeddings are already L2-normalized,
in which case dot product and cosine similarity produce identical rankings.

Euclidean (L2) distance measures the straight-line distance between two
points in vector space. Smaller distances mean more similar vectors. L2
distance is sensitive to both the direction and the magnitude of vectors,
and is commonly used in classic nearest-neighbor search and clustering
algorithms such as k-means and Annoy/Faiss L2 indexes.

Choosing a metric affects retrieval ranking, especially when embedding
magnitudes vary across documents (e.g. short vs. long chunks). In practice
cosine similarity is the safest default for text; dot product is preferred
for maximum inner product search (MIPS) at scale; and Euclidean distance is
common in image and audio embedding spaces.
